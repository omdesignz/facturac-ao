# Launch series: three directions for the four old teasers

The four films keep their own ideas: **O Endereço** (type "facturação" into the
address bar, fix it into facturac.ao), **O Ponto** (the gold dot lands between
"c" and "ao"), **Tudo acaba em .ao** (every "-ção" word a business lives this
year turns into ".ao") and **Mudar** (switching programs costs nothing: the
data comes along). What changes is where they happen: on a real phone, running
the real app, with each film ending in a launch call to action instead of a
teaser line. Every screen is captured from the product with demo data; nothing
on a phone is drawn.

## A. Um telefone, um dia (recommended)

Device: a moment in someone's day. Form: one continuous take per film, the
camera never leaving the phone's world. A real iPhone sits in a real place (a
shop counter, a workshop bench, a café table) and the owner's thumb does the
work; each film's idea happens *on* the phone instead of beside it.

- **Endereço:** Safari, the owner types "facturação", the address bar refuses
  and shakes; "Tire o til. Tire a cedilha. Ponha um ponto." happens key by key;
  the page opens straight into the app's Hoje.
- **O Ponto:** the word fills the screen; the til flies, the cedilha drops, and
  the gold dot bounces down into the home screen, becoming the app icon's dot.
  A tap opens Hoje.
- **Tudo acaba em .ao:** each word arrives as what the phone really shows:
  *atenção* is a notification ("venceu há 3 dias"), *migração* is the import,
  *comunicação* is an invoice going Emitida, Validada, Paga.
- **Mudar:** the old program's export lands as a file; the app reads it,
  numbering continues without a gap, and the seal stamps the first invoice.

First two seconds: a phone face-up on a counter, the screen lit, a thumb
already typing "facturação". Beats: need → refusal or old way → fix → the real
app performing → end card with the call to action. Look: the app's white
canvas, Century Gothic titles, the gold dot as the one carrier between films,
a photographed phone at full size with real reflections and contact shadow.
Sound: the existing tracks re-cut, with real key taps, haptics and the
notification chime as the punctuation.

## B. O ponto viaja

Device: a character does the work (the gold dot). Form: an object-to-object
chain. The dot from the logo is the one moving thing across all four films: it
falls into a phone and becomes the unread badge, the toggle that turns gold, the
status dot on "Validada", the full stop on the wordmark. Phones float at full
size on flat brand-colour fields, cut on the beat. More graphic and the
cheapest to vary for trades or seasons; less human.

First two seconds: the dot drops from the top of the frame and lands on a
phone's notification. Look: flat gold and ink fields, big type, phones
straight-on. Sound: one percussive track across the series, the dot's landings
on the beat.

## C. Luanda real (bold)

Device: life before and after, in real places. Form: split screen to one. The
old way plays out on one side (a receipt book, a spreadsheet, a queue) while
the phone on the other side does the same job; the split collapses into the
phone. The strongest version needs real photographs or footage of real
businesses and hands; without them it becomes stock-like and weaker than A.

First two seconds: a carbon receipt book being torn on the left, the same sale
appearing on a phone on the right. Look: photographic, daylight, the app's
white UI as the clean side. Sound: room tone and real sounds on the old side,
the track entering when the split collapses.
